# Assignment 1 - Exploring an AI Tool for Information Work 

## Objective
To gain practical experience with an AI tool relevant to your professional interests, and to reflect critically on its potential and limitations in information practice.

## Instructions

### 1. Choose a Tool
- Select one AI tool related to your professional or career interests  
  (e.g., a library discovery system, metadata tool, chatbot, recommendation system, or generative AI tool used in scholarly communication).

### 2. Learn & Demonstrate
- Explore the tool hands-on and learn its basic functionality.
- Create a 2-3 minute demo video showing:
  - What the tool does
  - How you used it for a small example task
  - What you noticed about its performance

### 3. Write a Short Reflection (≈100 words)
- The reflection should address:
  - What worked well and what didn’t
  - What benefits or limitations you observed
  - How you could see (or not see) this tool fitting into information work in your field

## Deliverables
- A 2-3 minute demo video (upload to Canvas)
- A ~100-word written reflection (upload to Canvas)

## Grading Rubric (15 points total)

### 1. Tool Selection & Relevance (3 points)
- The selected AI tool is clearly identified and relevant to the student’s professional or career interests.
- The choice makes sense in an information, library, or ICT-related context.

### 2. Demo Video: Clarity & Demonstration (6 points)
- The video clearly explains what the tool does.
- The student demonstrates a small, concrete example task using the tool.
- The demonstration is understandable and focused (no need for technical depth or polished production).

### 3. Reflection: Critical Observation (6 points)
- The reflection is grounded in the student’s hands-on use of the tool.
- It clearly discusses what worked well and what did not.
- It identifies at least one benefit and one limitation.
- It thoughtfully considers how (or whether) the tool could fit into information work in the student’s field.

> **Note:**  
> Grading emphasizes clarity, engagement, and critical reflection rather than technical sophistication or prior experience with AI tools.
